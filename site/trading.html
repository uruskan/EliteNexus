Trading, mining & colonisation

Make every tonne count.

Find a buyer for the hold you already have. Plan the next profitable run. Catch unusual prices as commanders report them. Your cargo, your ship and the community’s market data in one place.

Start with your next sale

Your hold. Your ship. The best known buyers.

A local market database, fed live by EDDN, your own journal and, if you like, Spansh’s daily dump, so every station is known from day one. Prices arrive as commanders report them, and their age stays visible, because a live feed can’t make every station’s last report current.

Sell my cargo

The best single stop for your whole hold, with what it pays and how much it will buy, re-planned after every jump; a second view gives the best buyer for each commodity. Station demand limits the estimate, and mission cargo, stolen goods and limpets stay out of the plan.

Find commodity

Type a name and pick from the suggestions (“ltd” finds Low Temperature Diamonds, “thar pro” Thargoid Probe). Each commodity gets its own page: the galactic average, where to buy, where to sell and the latest prices, filtered by distance up to the whole galaxy, landing pad, price age, stock or demand, carriers and planetary ports. Click a row for that market’s details and the commodity’s price history there.

An empty list explains itself: it names the nearest market outside your filters, says which filter left it out and widens them in one click. Your last commodity, filters and tab are there next time.

Build the next run

A plan that starts with your actual capacity.

Trade routes uses your ship’s cargo capacity and the credits you have, so every opportunity is one you can carry and afford.

  1. Choose the area

    Search around your current position, within up to 1,000 ly, with buying-to-selling hops up to 500 ly. Wide areas take a few seconds.

  2. Set the stops you can use

    Choose a landing pad requirement and whether to include fleet carriers. Compare the buying station, the selling station, the commodity and the expected profit.

  3. Check the report, then fly

    Use the listed quantities, distance and data age to judge the run. Community prices are reports of a market, so stock and demand may change before you arrive.

Pin it, plan it

Fill the orders you picked, in the right order.

Pin any market entry, from a commodity page, Abnormal prices or a station’s market. Pins stay on top of the Market page with their latest price, distance and age, saved between runs.

A run built from your pins

The Run planner finds where to buy what each pinned buyer wants (cargo already aboard first, then a pinned seller, then the nearest or cheapest seller within the distance you set) and the best buyer for each pinned seller. It fits your free hold space and credits, most profit per tonne first, and orders the stops so everything is bought before it is sold, on the shortest trip.

It shows profit, spend, every stop and what it had to leave out and why. Follow this run puts the stops on the Navigation page.

Is the carrier still there?

Fleet carriers move, and market reports age. In a pinned carrier’s system, its pin says here or not here, from the carriers the game has listed since you arrived (it lists them as they appear in your navigation panel; until then the pin says not listed yet).

The Station tab lists those carriers with their markets, plus any the database still places in the system that the game hasn’t listed. Click the tab again for the market of the station you’re docked at.

Find the outliers

Spot the offer you would never have searched for.

Abnormal prices scans the whole database for stations and fleet carriers paying far above a commodity’s galactic average, or selling far below it: a carrier buy order paying 100× the normal price, for example.

Look for the opportunity, not just a commodity

Pick the multiple (1.5× to 10×), the whole galaxy or a radius, and how old prices may be; the list updates as you change them. The price sits beside its galactic average, the quantity, the location, the distance and the age of the report. Carriers report no average, so they are compared with what stations report for that commodity.

High numbers need context

A spectacular price is useful only if the order has enough demand and the journey is worthwhile. Elite Nexus puts those details beside the offer so you can decide before loading up. A station that produces a good lists it with a token demand of 1; that doesn’t count as a buyer.

Let the market come to you

Set your price. Keep flying.

Alerts with your conditions

Create a buy or sell alert for a commodity, a price threshold and a distance, with a minimum stock or demand: Tritium selling for at least 60,000 Cr within 50 ly, say. A matching market report triggers it seconds after any commander docks there, spoken if you enable voice.

The feed, and what you share

Database & feed shows the live feed and offers the one-time Spansh import (4.5 GB streamed, nothing saved but the database). You receive community market data without enabling uploads; sharing your own market visits with EDDN is a separate, opt-in switch, with a pause for a market you’d rather keep to yourself for now.

From the ring to the construction site

The same market data, beyond trading.

Mining: a hotspot, a session, a buyer

Search known mining hotspots by mineral, distance and hotspot count, with the best sell prices alongside. Hotspots come from shared DSS ring scans and your own discoveries. The Mining page follows the live session: refined tonnes, tonnes per hour, prospect quality, motherlodes and earlier sessions. For Rhino surface mining it lists every planetary mining location you worked, with the body and the number to pick in the navigation panel to go back, what came out there and a note of your own; a surface radar points to the deposits you refined at and to your ship.

Colonisation: a depot’s needs as the next cargo run

Your constructions (depots in a system you claimed, or delivered to) show the remaining tonnage per commodity and how much of each you already carry. Where to buy finds the nearest stations with enough stock for a full hold of each, around the construction site, with price, stock and report age.

The game records a depot only while you’re docked there, so every figure is from your last visit; one not updated for four weeks is marked out of date. Depots you only docked at, most likely other commanders’ projects, are kept apart, and any depot can be hidden.

Finish the trip

Find the service you came for.

Services lists the nearest raw, manufactured and encoded material traders, human and Guardian technology brokers, Interstellar Factors, Universal Cartographics and Vista Genomics, as well as shipyards, outfitting and black markets. It searches as soon as it opens, when you pick a service or pad, and after every jump, and says so when no known station has the service. Every list on the Market page sorts by the column you click and remembers it.
