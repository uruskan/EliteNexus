Glare & exposure

Find the light you want to fly in.

Tame bright-light glare and lift dark tones with Odyssey’s own tone-mapping settings. Start with a preset, refine the picture and keep a direct path back to the game’s defaults.

Why everything outside looks dark

Take control of how the scene is drawn.

Odyssey’s auto-exposure reacts to the cockpit’s own lights: with a bright dashboard in view, everything outside is drawn darker, and the adaptation can’t be switched off. What players change instead are three tone-mapping values in the game’s settings file, and this tab puts them in one place.

Glare from lights

The game’s GlareCompensation, 1.33 by default. Lower means less glare around bright light sources.

Standard ACES curve

Draws darks and mid-tones brighter than the game’s own curve and leaves highlights the same. The page shows by how much.

Auto-exposure targets

Under the advanced controls, the three percentiles the exposure is set from. All of these change the whole picture: space, stations and planets alike.

From preset to your next flight

Choose. Apply. Restart.

  1. Pick a preset and adjust

    Open HUD & cockpit › Glare and exposure, start from a preset and fine-tune the controls to your display.

  2. Apply to the game

    Elite Nexus writes the values into the game’s GraphicsConfigurationOverride.xml, the same file HUD colours uses, and keeps everything else in it. No graphics hook is involved.

  3. Compare after a restart

    The game reads these values only at start, so quit to the desktop and launch it again. To go back, apply Game default and restart once more.

Good to know

An experiment, with a way back.

Does this switch off Odyssey’s auto-exposure?

No. It can’t be switched off; these values change the glare and the tone curve the adaptation works with. Frontier doesn’t document them, so treat the presets as starting points and compare the result in the scenes you actually fly through.

How is this different from Bloom?

Bloom, in the game’s Graphics options, sets how much bright lights glow. Glare compensation and the tone curve are separate values, so adjust them alongside your preferred Bloom setting.

Will this dim the cockpit lamps themselves?

No. The lamps can’t be dimmed this way; this tab changes how the whole picture is drawn. Dimming the cockpit’s own lights is Cockpit lights’ job, and HUD brightness has its own control in HUD colours.

Does it work on Linux and on a Mac?

The values live in the game’s Options folder, which on Linux and macOS sits inside its Proton, Wine, CrossOver or Whisky prefix, where Elite Nexus finds it.
