Odyssey exobiology

Find the planet. Finish the samples.

Choose promising worlds before you land. Know when you’ve travelled far enough for the next sample. Keep your progress and unsold value in view from the first biological signal to Vista Genomics.

Live sampling radar

Stop guessing the distance to your next sample.

The radar is turned to your heading, so up is the way you’re facing. Every previous sample shows its colony-range circle, and a big readout says OK to sample or too close, with how much farther to move.

  1. Take the first sample

    The species, its value, its colony range and your three-sample progress appear as you use the genetic sampler.

  2. Move beyond the colony range

    See the distance to your nearest sample and the spacing still to cover. The radar keeps the previous sample positions in view as you turn and move.

  3. Look for green

    The indicator turns green once you are far enough from the recorded samples. Complete the analysis and the species joins your unsold data.

Keep it over the game

The overlay at the top of the game’s screen says how many samples you have and whether you’re far enough for the next one to count, or how far to walk, and opens with the radar when you take a sample. Run Elite Nexus while you sample so it can record each sample’s surface position.

Know what to look for

Turn biological signals into a landing decision.

This system lists the bodies with life, with their signal counts, gravity, temperature and atmosphere, and the genera found on them. Species predictions give you a lead before you commit to the surface.

From a prediction to an identification

FSS scans reveal the bodies and their biological signals; DSS mapping identifies the genera. From a body’s atmosphere, gravity, temperature, volcanism and stars, Elite Nexus predicts the likely species with their values and sampling ranges, kept visibly apart from species you have actually sampled.

Keep each body organised

See what is still predicted, which species has samples in progress and which analysis is complete, so you can move between worlds without remembering every signal and sample count.

Strategy one · Bonus hunt

Search for a chance at the first-logged payout.

Target a valuable species, Stratum Tectonicas by default, and Elite Nexus searches Spansh for planets whose conditions suit it, then spells out what it found: how many planets suit the species, how many already have samples on record, were mapped without the genus, are in populated systems or were landed on, and which are left.

No record

Nobody sharing data has mapped or sampled it. The species is predicted, so you are choosing a promising candidate rather than a confirmed location: your best chance.

Mapped, no samples

Someone mapped it, so the genus is confirmed, but no sample is on record. The first-logged bonus may already be gone; these candidates are hidden by default.

Refine the search, then tour the best

Filters for evidence, predicted chance, distance from the arrival star and gravity apply instantly. Tour the best 15 plans a route through your selected prospects, nearest first, and hands it to Navigation.

A lead, with its evidence attached

Records come only from players whose tools share data through EDDN, and a sample leaves one only when it adds a codex entry for that player. No record is a strong lead, not proof that nobody has visited; the further from the bubble, the more you find.

Strategy two · Sure value

Follow species commanders have already found.

Prefer a route with known targets? Sure value is Spansh’s exobiology planner: planets where commanders already logged valuable species.

Choose your payout threshold

Set a minimum species value, a search radius and a number of stops, then review the systems, planets, recorded species and base values before following the route.

Expect the base value

Someone has already logged these species, so the payout is certain but the first-logged bonus has most likely been claimed. Both tours go to Navigation, where arriving advances your progress.

From fieldwork to payday

Keep the value of the expedition in sight.

Unsold Vista Genomics data lists completed species and their expected value, and your totals track species analysed, credits already sold and Exobiologist rank. Market › Services finds the nearest Vista Genomics when it’s time to turn it in.

The first-logged bonus, only where it can be paid

The ×5 bonus is shown only in an uninhabited system, on a body nobody had set foot on when you first scanned it, for a species not marked as logged. The game’s own “not logged” flag alone doesn’t mean the bonus is coming: in one commander’s sales it was set for dozens of species and the bonus came for two, never in a populated system. A species nobody sold on an already-visited body can still pay it; that shows up as a good surprise at Vista Genomics rather than a promise the sale breaks.
