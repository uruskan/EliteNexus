Exploration & navigation

Know what’s worth the detour.

See the value in the system around you, choose the next worlds to scan and follow the journey without losing track of your discoveries. Exploration, travel planning and your galaxy map work together.

Read the system at a glance

Decide what deserves a closer look.

As you scan, Elite Nexus builds the system as a tree of stars, planets and moons, with each body’s value now and its value if mapped side by side (first discovery and mapping, the Odyssey bonus, efficiency), and the highlights worth a detour: Earth-likes, water and ammonia worlds, terraformables, landable atmospheres, close and wide rings, shepherd and nested moons, fast rotation, jumponium.

The next decision is in the detail

Click a body for its gravity, temperature and atmosphere, materials, rings and hotspots, biology and geology signals, and discovery and footfall status from your journal. Decide whether a long flight to a planet is worth the extra mapping value before you leave the arrival star. The Codex keeps your recorded discoveries within reach.

Honest about empty systems

In a system you haven’t scanned, or a populated one where scanning pays little, the page says so instead of showing zeros. Optional spoken alerts announce valuable bodies and biological signals as you scan.

Find valuable bodies

Make a scanning trip worth taking.

Find valuable bodies is Spansh’s Road to Riches: a tour of Earth-likes, water and ammonia worlds and terraformables, up to 1,000 ly and 2,000 stops.

  1. Choose how you want to explore

    Rank by the FSS scan alone or by the value after DSS mapping. Set the minimum value, the arrival distance and the search area to suit the time you want to spend.

  2. Review the worlds on the route

    See the systems, the target bodies and their estimated values before committing.

  3. Follow it in Navigation

    Follow this route sends the tour to Navigation, where stops tick off as you arrive and the next system can go to your clipboard after every jump.

Starting in the bubble?

Spansh skips populated systems, so from the bubble an empty search widens itself, to 100, 250, 500, then 1,000 ly, until it finds something, and says so.

Navigation

One place to follow every journey.

Your in-game route, Spansh travel plans and the tours from Exploration, Exobiology or the assistant come together in Navigation: star classes, scoopable stops, neutron and white-dwarf hazards, the longest stretch without a scoopable star, and the next system copied to your clipboard after every jump, once you switch that on.

Neutron routes

Travel through neutron stars using your jump range, with Spansh’s routing efficiency in the options.

Exact ship routes

Every jump planned from your ship’s FSD, mass, fuel and cargo, with the routing strategy, neutron boosts, injections and refuelling choices one click away.

Fleet carrier routes

Planned with your carrier’s tritium and cargo (or your squadron carrier’s, with Spansh’s squadron figures), with the carrier’s live jump timer beside it. With a carrier route active, the next leg and an estimate of the time to the destination are shown, and stops tick off as the carrier arrives, wherever you are.

Your galaxy map

See how far you’ve come. Choose where to go.

The Milky Way with everywhere you’ve been, your travel path, the active route, your carriers, parked ships and landmarks such as Sol, Colonia, Sagittarius A* and Beagle Point.

A map you can use

Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom; jump to where you are, the whole galaxy or all your travels, and choose the layers to show. Search finds any system you’ve visited or a landmark. Click a system for its distance from you, to copy its name, or to plot a route to it in Navigation.

Bring the discoveries home

Know what is still waiting to be sold.

Unsold data totals your cartographic discoveries, with current and mapped values by system. History keeps the wider record: every system you scanned and what became of the data.

A history that explains the totals

Filter systems by sold (and when), unsold, not sellable or lost. Scans Universal Cartographics left out of a sale and data lost with your ship are identified separately, so they don’t quietly inflate your unsold total.

Who has set foot there?

Footfall for the bodies in your current system comes from your journal. For a planet in another system, ask the assistant (“has anyone landed on planet X in system Y?”).
