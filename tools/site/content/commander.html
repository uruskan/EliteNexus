Dashboard & overlay

Everything at a glance.

Commander, ranks and progress, credits and legal state, your ship (fuel, hull, cargo, jump range, rebuy), location and system politics, session earnings, the current system’s exploration value, holdings, your carriers with their jump countdowns, colonisation and an activity feed.

Follow the session

Earnings and activity since you logged in. Transfers to your own carrier aren’t counted as spending; credits given to a squadron carrier are, because they’re the squadron’s.

An island over the game

The overlay is one small black island at the top of the game’s screen. It says what’s going on in a word or two (“2 of 3 samples · Far enough”, “Scoopable · 12 jumps left”), opens up for the moments that need you, such as the next jump, an arrival worth it, an approach, a sample or docking, and fades away when nothing does. Clicks go through it, it never takes the focus from the game, and it shows only while the game is in front.

The rest of your kit

Odyssey suits and weapons, synthesis, your materials and your ship locker live on the same page.

Ships & carriers

Know what you own. Know where it is.

Fleet lists every ship and where it is, in transit included, with its full loadout, engineering and modifier changes. Fleet carrier follows your own carrier and your squadron’s.

Inspect the exact build

Open a ship for its modules and engineering, then open the exact build in EDSY or Coriolis with one click.

Follow your carriers

Tritium, finances, capacity, crew and services, orders, the jump schedule and history for your fleet carrier and, if you have one, your squadron’s (a switch picks which). The Jump card counts down to a scheduled jump (time left, destination, departure, until when you can cancel, when the lockdown starts), then the cooldown until the carrier can jump again. A decommission shows its scrap date and refund.

Everything but the position and jumps comes from carrier management, which the game writes when you open that screen, so the page says how old it is. The journal records only the jumps and orders you make yourself; for a squadron carrier, other members’ changes show after you next open it.

Missions, BGS & Powerplay

See the work still ahead.

Track the mission stack

Active missions with expiry times, cargo deliveries and kill progress. The massacre-stack tracker counts a kill once for every issuing faction, so you see the true kills remaining.

Review BGS & Powerplay activity

What you did for which faction and power, with a view since the last tick. Set the tick hour your group uses; it and the period you pick are remembered. Fleet carrier transactions stay out, since carriers are outside the BGS.

Logbook & statistics

Find what happened. See where it’s going.

Search the logbook

Activity, every journal event with its JSON and full-text search, travel, docking and a credit ledger. Search finds a system, station or commander name anywhere in your history.

Read your statistics

Credits over time (with a log scale, so a climb from millions to billions stays readable), income and spending by category, jumps and distance per day, deaths, and the game’s own statistics, over 7, 30 or 90 days or all time.

Controls

Keep the right action within reach.

Your active presets per category, conflicts, missing essentials and a searchable list of every bound action. Read-only: Elite Nexus never changes your bindings; change them in the game, under Options › Controls.

Find an action or a conflict

Search your bindings and review conflicts and missing essentials. When a preset the game uses can’t be read, say a built-in one while the game’s folder wasn’t found, a warning names the preset shown instead and why, and offers to choose the game folder.

Keep a reference beside you

Printable reference card writes an HTML card of your controls and opens it, ready to print or keep nearby.
