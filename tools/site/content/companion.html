AI co-pilot

Ask from where you are.

Ask answers plain questions from your ship, cargo, journal, the market database and Spansh, with a built-in knowledge base of game mechanics and the user guide. Nothing else in the app needs it.

Find a useful next job

Where to sell your cargo, price deals, trade routes, mining hotspots, the nearest services, or where to make money with this ship in the next hour. Results depend on the data the app has.

Look beyond the next jump

Systems and their planets, who has set foot on them, untouched biology, the exobiology around you, or an exploration or exobiology trip planned through Spansh. Record age and coverage matter, especially for first footfall and bonus hunting.

Turn an answer into a route

Routes the assistant plans land on Navigation, where the stops tick off as you travel. Journal search finds something from an earlier session.

Useful questions to start with

“Where should I sell my cargo?” · “Plan an exobiology trip near me.” · “Where can I make the most money in the next hour?”

The assistant can make mistakes. Check the destination, the data age and the route before committing to a trip.

Choose your model

Connect the provider that suits you.

Google Gemini is the default: click Get a free Gemini key, paste it, Connect. Other providers and model offers Claude or a model running on your own PC.

Google Gemini

Your own API key. Availability, limits, pricing and data terms are Google’s.

Anthropic Claude

Your own API key, billed to your Anthropic account under its pricing and data terms.

A model on your PC

Run a tool-capable model in Ollama or LM Studio, choose Local model and give its address. The model server must be running before Ask can answer.

What a model receives

With a cloud provider, your questions, the conversation, your commander context and the tool results used for the answer go to that provider. A local model keeps inference on your PC, while tools that query Spansh or other online services still make network requests.

Alerts & voice

Hear what matters while you fly.

Alerts appear as banners and, if you enable voice, are spoken: on Windows through Windows speech or SAPI (which also has the voices other programs install, such as IVONA or CereProc) in the voice you pick, through speech-dispatcher on Linux or the system voice on a Mac. Choose the lowest priority you want to hear.

What gets announced

Hull critical and ship destroyed; Earth-like, water, ammonia, terraformable and other high-value bodies; motherlodes; analysis complete; a massacre mission done; fuel low with a non-scoopable star next; interdiction; biological signals and the distance to move after a first sample; codex entries; a scheduled carrier jump; arrivals and scan counts.

Price alerts, spoken

Market price alerts are spoken seconds after any commander’s report matches them. Test voice in Settings says which voice spoke, or why none could.

Data & sync

Choose what you share.

Settings, the market database and logs live in Elite Nexus’s own data folder, and each community service has its own switch.

Enable each service on its own

EDDN (schema-checked, with a test mode for your first session), EDSM journal sync, Inara profile sync and EDAstro are opt-in. Inara approved the app by name in October 2026, so your personal API key is all it needs; the section confirms who the key belongs to. Each card shows sent, queued and failed counts and why a service is paused; unsent items survive restarts, and switching a service off discards its queue.

Community sync and cloud AI are separate

The sync switches control community uploads; they don’t affect what Ask sends to the model provider you chose. Pick a local model if you want inference on your PC.

Settings & recovery

Found by itself. Never silently reset.

Folders found for you

The journal folder and the game folder are found automatically: every Steam library (Steam on another drive included), the Epic and Frontier folders, and on Linux and macOS the game’s Proton, Wine, CrossOver or Whisky prefix. Either can be typed, pasted or picked instead, and a journal folder on another PC over the network works.

Settings that survive a mistake

If the settings file can’t be read, typically after editing it by hand, Elite Nexus keeps the unreadable file under a dated name, keeps every setting that still reads, and says so in a banner above every page. Nothing is lost without a word.

A crash leaves a trail

If Elite Nexus ever closes unexpectedly, it writes where to crash.log in its data folder and says so at the next start. Neither it nor nexus.log is sent anywhere; attaching them to a GitHub issue or a forum post helps fix the problem.
