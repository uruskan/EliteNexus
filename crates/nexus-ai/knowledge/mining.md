# Mining

## Methods
- Laser mining: shoot asteroids with mining lasers, collect the fragments with collector limpets, refine them in the refinery. Works for Platinum, Painite, Low Temperature Diamonds, Osmium, Tritium and most metals.
- Core (deep core) mining: find motherlode asteroids with the pulse wave analyser, place seismic charges in the fissures, detonate, collect the chunks. Yields Void Opals, Low Temperature Diamonds, Alexandrite, Grandidierite, Monazite, Musgravite, Rhodplumsite, Serendibite, Benitoite and Bromellite.
- Sub-surface deposits: blast surface deposits with the sub-surface displacement missile, then the abrasion blaster.

## Hotspots and rings
A detailed surface scan of a planetary ring shows hotspots: concentrations of one commodity. Overlapping hotspots (two or more of the same commodity) give much faster yields. Metallic rings: Platinum, Painite, Palladium, Gold, Osmium. Icy rings: Low Temperature Diamonds, Void Opals, Tritium, Bromellite. Rocky and metal-rich rings: Alexandrite, Monazite, Grandidierite, Serendibite, Benitoite and others. Elite Nexus' Market > Mining hotspots tab finds known hotspots near the commander together with the best places to sell.

## Prospecting and efficiency
Fire a prospector limpet at an asteroid first: it shows the material content (and "motherlode" for core asteroids). Only mine asteroids with a high percentage of the target material. Fit enough collector limpet controllers, a large refinery and plenty of cargo racks. Elite Nexus tracks tonnes per hour and motherlodes on the Mining page.

## Surface mining with the Rhino
Since September 2026 (game update 4.4.1) the MEV Rhino SRV mines planet surfaces. It carries a deposit scanner, mining rigs and a refinery. Scan a planet with the detailed surface scanner: its Planetary Mining Locations appear in the navigation panel, numbered. Target one, land by it and deploy the Rhino. Drive with the deposit scanner held to find deposits, put mining rigs down on a deposit, then collect: the refinery turns what the rigs dig into cargo, by-products included, which you transfer to the ship. The game warns when you drive about 4 km from a rig and a rig left more than 5 km behind is destroyed; boarding the ship or relogging loses the rigs you left out. Rigs need about 75 m between them (Frontier hasn't published it; that's what commanders reported in September 2026, estimates 75 to 100 m) and commanders report up to six working at once, so a round deposit takes 2 rigs from 75 m across, 3 from 87 m, 4 from 106 m, 5 from 128 m and 6 from 150 m (one in the middle, five on a ring 75 m out). Deposits run from a few metres to 300 m across (the codex, as players quote it); a rig extracts up to 12 chunks (update 4.4.1.1). A deposit has a density and an amount that runs down as you mine it; the HUD shows them, the journal doesn't. One commander reported about 500 t in a high-density deposit, 800 to 900 t medium and 1,800 t low, refilling over weeks once mined out. The update added minable commodities: Iridium, Helium, Helium-3, Bastnasite, Deuterium, Thortveitite, Quartz Pyroxenite, Olivine, Periclase Dunite, Sapphire, Diamond, Ruby and Magnesite.

Elite Nexus's Mining page lists every mining location the Rhino refined at (Surface mining sites): the system, the body, the location's number as the navigation panel shows it, the tonnes per commodity, visits and a note of your own. To go back, jump to the system, target the body and pick that location number in the navigation panel. The number is the location the game names when your ship touches down or lifts off within 50 km of one; a Rhino dropped from a hovering ship takes the mining location you had targeted instead, recorded only while Elite Nexus runs. The Surface radar on the Mining page shows the deposits you refined at, your ship and its last landing spot with distance and compass bearing, scaled to what's near you (on the page, anything further out waits on the rim with its distance; on the overlay, at its edge), with a ring round each deposit showing the ground the Rhino refined on there (where the rigs most likely stood), a north-up minimap of the deposits, the ship and the commander, and a Placing rigs guide; and in the Rhino the overlay names the nearest deposit, how far and which way; deposit positions are only recorded while Elite Nexus is running. Deposits are numbered on each body in the order they were found (the numbers are kept), and each takes a note of your own in the list under the radar, such as how many rigs fit, to tell two deposits of the same commodity apart.

Where to surface-mine: the Mining page's Where to mine tab. Near you lists landable bodies with planetary mining locations within 25 to 1,000 ly (from Spansh), with each body's ground, number of mining locations, distance from the star and gravity, and plans a tour through the nearest systems. Along a route finds the system with the most mining locations in each stretch of the trip to a destination and puts the stops on the Navigation page. A body's ground is its class (metal-rich, high metal content, rocky, rocky ice or icy) and its volcanism (none, metallic magma, rocky magma, silicate magma, silicate vapour geysers, water magma, water geysers, carbon dioxide geysers, ammonia, methane or nitrogen magma), as the game reports them; the search filters on either or both, or on a material (then only the classes where it turned up, each body with its odds). Your grounds shows what the commander's own Rhino runs gave on each ground. Counts of mining locations come from scans commanders share through EDDN, and only scans since September 2026 carry them, so a body without a count may still have locations. Nothing in the journal or any feed says what a mining location holds: the game records what the refinery made, not what a deposit contained, so never promise a commodity on a body; a ground, its odds and the commander's own runs are leads. In the game, targeting a location after a DSS scan shows its materials in the target panel, so advise checking there before landing.

## Selling mined goods
Mining income depends almost entirely on where it is sold: prices differ by millions of credits per hold between stations and change daily. Use "Sell my cargo" or the best-sell list before leaving the ring. Large amounts can exceed one station's demand; split the sale or pick a station with high demand.

## Good mining ships
Large: Type-9, Type-10, Imperial Cutter, Anaconda, Panther Clipper Mk II. Medium: Python, Krait Mk II, Type-8. Small starter: Cobra Mk III or Asp Explorer with a couple of mining lasers. Bring a fuel scoop or a carrier nearby.
