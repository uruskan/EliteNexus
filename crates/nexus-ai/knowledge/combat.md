# Combat and missions

These are the rules as of game version 4.4 (November 2026). Frontier's Law & Disorder update, due in December 2026, rewrites crime and punishment: crimes count only once the authorities confirm them, the Kill Warrant Scanner and Interstellar Factors are removed, inhabited anarchy systems get enforcer ships, notoriety becomes criminal tiers, and a pledged commander killing another Power's ships stops being a crime. Elite Nexus' Combat page lists the nearest wars (Fights near you) and explains these rules (How to fight legally).

## Where killing is legal
- Ships shown as Wanted in the current jurisdiction: target them and let the scan finish first. A ship with bounties only elsewhere ("Warrant") isn't a legal target; a Kill Warrant Scanner reveals bounties from other jurisdictions and pays them all on a kill.
- The other side in a conflict zone you've joined. Nothing you destroy while officially participating adds notoriety.
- Thargoids: each kill pays a combat bond; they belong to no faction.
- Anarchy systems (controlled by an anarchy faction): no fines, no bounties, no police; destroying clean ships there adds no notoriety, and the Pilots' Federation bounty for killing a player doesn't apply. On foot, a settlement of a non-anarchy faction keeps its law.
- Anything else, a clean ship, a station, the police, is a crime.

## Conflict zones
Wars and civil wars (not elections) create conflict zones in space (low, medium or high intensity; a capital ship can appear only in high) and at settlements on foot. They show in the navigation panel in supercruise within about 1,000 ls. When you drop in, choose a side in the Comms panel before firing. Kills pay combat bonds from your side: hand them in at a station your side controls, or at Interstellar Factors anywhere for a 25% cut; bonds are lost if the ship is destroyed. On foot, join at the Frontline Solutions desk in a port of that system (pick the settlement and side, then the dropship), or land near the settlement and accept the Comms-panel prompt. Ships destroyed in conflict zones drop no materials.

## How a war is decided
A war (at least one faction from another system) or civil war (both native) starts when two factions reach equal influence. It lasts up to seven days: each day goes to the side with the higher score at the tick (conflict zones won, bonds, combat missions, bounties), and it ends once the trailing side can't catch up, usually when one side has won four days. Even days at the end is a draw. The winner takes the loser's stake, its most valuable asset (a station or settlement). A pending war is declared but not yet fought; Frontier doesn't say how long that takes (players report one to three days). Elections have no conflict zones: they're won with non-combat missions, trade, exploration data and search and rescue.

## Bounty hunting
Wanted ships gather at resource extraction sites (low, normal, high, hazardous) and nav beacons. System security helps, except at hazardous sites and compromised nav beacons. Bounties are paid at a station of the issuing faction (security contact) or at Interstellar Factors for any faction, for a 25% cut.

## Massacre and assassination missions
Pirate massacre targets are Wanted, so killing them is legal. Several factions often offer massacre missions against the same target faction: each kill counts for one mission from every issuing faction at once, so accepting missions from many different factions (up to 20 missions in total) multiplies the payout. Elite Nexus' Missions page shows the real number of kills still needed across the stack. Some missions ask for illegal kills; the mission details usually say so (not always). A clean target is murder whoever asked. Massacre missions for a side in a war are done in its conflict zones.

## Powerplay
Pledged commanders earn merits for killing ships of rival Powers in acquisition, reinforcement and undermining systems; until December 2026 Frontier calls this technically a crime (players report small bounties, no notoriety). Crimes between pledged commanders bring a Power bounty: no police respond, only that Power's pledges can claim it, and it never sends anyone to a detention centre. Power conflict zones form in contested systems: merits per kill, bonds cashed only at the Power's stronghold carrier.

## Operations
Since update 4.4 (June 2026), Operations are squad fights for up to four, started from the ship panel while docked, in Mercenary or Powerplay mode. Bounties, fines and bonds are set aside before you go in, and nothing done there adds notoriety. They pay credits, materials, Merc Coins and, in Powerplay mode, merits.

## Crimes, notoriety and the detention centre
- Fines: illegal or stolen cargo, loitering at a station, collisions at speed near a station, abandoned missions, light hits on a clean ship (reckless weapons discharge). Bounties: damaging or destroying clean ships or SRVs (destroying one is murder), killing people, trespassing, destroying property. Bounties never expire; unpaid fines can turn into bounties. Bounties with five or more factions of one superpower add a superpower bounty.
- Notoriety runs from 0 to 10: each murder adds 1 (less on foot), and it drops by 1 for every two hours logged in, docked included. Above 0, Interstellar Factors won't clear bounties, and murder bounties grow with each point.
- Pay bounties at Interstellar Factors (found in low-security and anarchy systems) once notoriety is 0, but not in a system where the issuing faction is present. Handing yourself in at a security contact also pays them and sends you to a detention centre.
- Destroyed (or killed on foot) where you're wanted, with the bounty claimed by police, a bounty hunter or a player: you go to a detention centre and pay your rebuy plus that jurisdiction's fines and bounties. Ships you committed crimes in are impounded and cost a release fee. If you can't pay, you get a Sidewinder.
- Attacking another player is a crime like any other unless they're Wanted, you're in an anarchy, or Powerplay rules apply; murdering a player also brings a Pilots' Federation bounty, valid everywhere but anarchies.

## Staying safe
- Keep the rebuy cost in credits.
- Interdictions: submitting (throttle to zero) gives a shorter FSD cooldown than losing the minigame; boost away and jump if outmatched.
- Heat, shields and module health: check after neutron boosts and combat.

## Thargoid and Guardian content
Fighting Thargoids needs anti-xeno (AX) weapons and modules, many of them Guardian technology unlocked from Guardian sites. The Thargoid war ended when the last Titan fell in December 3310 (2024); in 2025 Thargoids still held parts of the Pleiades, Witch Head, Coalsack and California nebulae.
