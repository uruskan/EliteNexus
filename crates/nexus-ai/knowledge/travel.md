# Travel and navigation

## Route planning: Spansh exact plotter vs in-game plotter
The in-game galaxy map plots routes with simple rules. Spansh's exact plotter (in Elite Nexus: Navigation > Exact plotter) plans every jump for the commander's real ship: actual frame shift drive, engineering, mass, fuel tank and cargo, refuelling stops at scoopable stars, and neutron or white-dwarf boosts. For long trips it is usually much faster and safer. Elite Nexus copies the next system of the planned route to the clipboard after every jump: open the galaxy map and paste it.
The neutron plotter (Navigation > Neutron) is quicker to compute and only needs the jump range; it follows neutron stars between two systems. It doesn't plan fuel, and its waypoints are a few jumps apart (the galaxy map plots the jumps between them), so for most trips the exact plotter is the better choice.

## Jump range
Jump range depends on the frame shift drive (class, rating, engineering), total mass (hull, modules, fuel, cargo) and the Guardian FSD Booster. To increase it: engineer the FSD with Increased FSD Range (grade 5) and the Mass Manager experimental, fit a Guardian FSD Booster, reduce mass (lightweight armour, smaller or D-rated modules, less fuel and cargo). The game reports the current maximum in the ship's Loadout; the app shows it in Fleet.

## Neutron stars and white dwarfs (supercharging)
Flying through the jet cone of a neutron star supercharges the frame shift drive: the next jump has four times the normal range. White dwarf cones give a 50 % boost. The cone damages the FSD a little and can throw the ship around; heat rises. Approach the star's pole along the cone, keep out of the exclusion zone, and repair the FSD with an AFM unit on long trips.

## FSD injections (jumponium)
Synthesis can boost a single jump: basic +25 %, standard +50 %, premium +100 %. The materials (Carbon, Vanadium, Germanium, Cadmium, Niobium, Arsenic, Yttrium, Polonium, …) are mostly found on planet surfaces. Useful to reach a system just out of range, or to escape regions with sparse stars.

## Fuel and scooping
Main-sequence stars of classes K, G, B, F, O, A and M can be scooped for fuel (remembered as "KGB FOAM"). Brown dwarfs, neutron stars, white dwarfs, black holes and T Tauri stars cannot. A larger fuel scoop refuels faster. Never let the tank run near empty in sparse regions; Elite Nexus warns when fuel is low and the next star is not scoopable. If stranded, the Fuel Rats rescue commanders for free.

## Supercruise and travel time
Supercruise assist flies to a target automatically. The SCO frame shift drives (Supercruise Overcharge) cut long supercruise trips dramatically at the cost of heat and fuel. Stations far from the arrival star (tens of thousands of light seconds) cost minutes; the market tools show each station's distance from the star (ls) so close options can be preferred.

## Landing pads and ship size
Stations have small, medium and large pads; outposts have no large pads, so large ships (Anaconda, Cutter, Corvette, Type-9, Type-10, Beluga, Panther Clipper Mk II, …) cannot dock there. Planetary ports and Odyssey settlements vary. The market tools filter by the pad size the commander's ship needs.

## Fleet carrier jumps
A fleet carrier jumps up to 500 ly at a time after a 15-minute countdown, burning tritium (more for a heavier carrier or a longer jump). Plan long carrier trips with the fleet carrier planner (Navigation > Fleet carrier), which counts tritium and refuel stops.
